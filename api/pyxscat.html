pyxscat: the kernels¶

PyXSCAT: ab initio X-ray and electron scattering from wavefunctions.

import pyxscat as xs wf = xs.interfaces.pyscf.from_cc(mycc) total = xs.scattering(wf, q, “total”)

Authors: Andrés Moreno Carrascosa, Mats Simmermacher, Patrick Yuheng Wang, Eirik Moen Liane, Joseph Cooper, Nikola Zotev, Thomas Northey and Adam Kirrander.

pyxscat.core¶

Scattering from a basis set and density matrices.

Nothing in this module depends on an electronic structure package. All density matrices are expressed in the contracted functions of the Basis (atomic units; q in inverse bohr). Conventions:

  • dm1[mu, nu] is the spin-summed one-particle density matrix, rho(r) = sum dm1[mu, nu] chi_mu(r) chi_nu(r).

  • dm2[mu, nu, la, si] is the spin-summed two-particle density matrix in chemists’ notation, Gamma(r1, r2) = sum dm2[mu, nu, la, si] chi_mu(r1) chi_nu(r1) chi_la(r2) chi_si(r2), normalised to N(N-1). This is the convention of PySCF’s make_rdm2.

class pyxscat.core.FactoredDM2(ac=None, bc=None, ax=None, bx=None)[source]¶

Bases: object

A two-particle density given as a sum of products of one-particle matrices,

Gamma[mu,nu,la,si] = sum_k Ac_k[mu,nu] Bc_k[la,si] + sum_k Ax_k[mu,la] Bx_k[nu,si]

(Coulomb and exchange pairings). The kernels symmetrise over the 8 index permutations, so only contractions with symmetric kernels are defined. Memory 2 K n^2 instead of n^4: the CCSD density of pyxscat.ccsd.factored_rdm2() has K = 7 nocc^2 + 4 terms. Weights are folded into the A matrices. transform(c) changes the basis of every matrix (M -> c M c^T); dense() builds the four-index array (small systems only).

pyxscat.core.symmetrize_dm2(dm2, inplace=False)[source]¶

Average a two-particle density matrix over its 8 index permutations.

The integrals are invariant under these permutations, so the scattering is unchanged; the kernel loops over symmetry-unique quartets and needs the symmetric form.

pyxscat.core.lsign(l)[source]¶

i^l for even l: the phase of the l-th term of the plane-wave expansion exp(i q.r) = sum_l (2l+1) i^l j_l(qr) P_l(q^.r^). Every l-moment returned by pyxscat includes it, so S2 = -sum Gamma <j2 P2>, and the moments add up with plus signs: I(q, theta_q) = sum_l (2l+1) <P_l(cos beta)> P_l(cos theta_q) S_l(q).

pyxscat.core.two_electron(basis, q, dm2, symmetrize=True, nthreads=None, return_stats=False, l=0, **cutoffs)[source]¶

Two-electron part of the total scattering, sum Gamma <ij|j0(q r12)|kr>.

Tends to N(N-1) at q = 0 and to zero at large q. Set symmetrize=False only if dm2 already has the full 8-fold permutational symmetry (saves one copy of the array).

l=2 gives the second Legendre moment about the molecular z axis, sum Gamma <ij| j2(q r12) P2(cos theta_12) |kr> (see scattering()).

pyxscat.core.elastic(basis, q, dm1, dm1_b=None, nthreads=None, return_stats=False, l=0, method='pairs', **cutoffs)[source]¶

Rotationally averaged elastic scattering, <|F(q)|^2>, from a one-particle density matrix.

With two different matrices this is the rotational average of F_a(q) F_b(q)^*, the building block of coherent mixed and inelastic terms from transition densities.

method

"pairs" (default): the elastic pair density is a product, so the density is folded into every primitive pair first and pairs of Hermite charge distributions are combined (xs_elastic.f90). Exact at coincident pair centres, rigorous q-dependent screening. "quartets": the general shell-quartet kernel with the pair density built on the fly (the one-loop algorithm; kept for comparison).

pyxscat.core.scattering(wf, q, kind='total', nthreads=None, l=0, **cutoffs)[source]¶

Rotationally averaged X-ray scattering of a Wavefunction.

kind

"total" N + two-electron term (needs wf.dm2 and wf.nelec) "elastic" <|F(q)|^2> from wf.dm1 "inelastic" total - elastic "two_electron" the two-electron term alone "hf_total" N + mean-field two-electron term from wf.dm1 only

l

0 (default): the isotropic signal, the average over all orientations. 2: the second Legendre moment about the molecular z axis, S2(q) = -sum Gamma <j2(q r12) P2(cos theta_12)> (the i^2 = -1 of the plane-wave expansion included, see lsign(); S2 = <I(q) P2(cos theta_q)> for perfect alignment). For molecules whose z axis is aligned along a laboratory axis (free rotation about it), the signal at a scattering vector making the angle theta_q with that axis is

I(q, theta_q) = I0(q) + 5 <P2(cos beta)> P2(cos theta_q) S2(q) + (l = 4, …)

with <P2(cos beta)> the degree of alignment (1 for perfect alignment). The isotropic one-electron term N does not contribute at l = 2.

pyxscat.core.decompose(wf, q, nthreads=None, l=0, **cutoffs)[source]¶

Split the total scattering into mean-field and correlation contributions.

Gamma = D D - 1/2 D D (exchange) + Lambda (cumulant), so that

total = N + hartree + exchange + cumulant, elastic = hartree.

Returns a dict of arrays over q. The mean-field parts are built from the 1RDM on the fly; only the full two-electron term uses the four-index array. l=2: the same split of the second Legendre moment (see scattering()); its self term is zero.

pyxscat.basis¶

Package-independent description of a Gaussian basis set.

The scattering kernels only ever see this object. Every contracted basis function is a linear combination of primitive Cartesian Gaussians:

chi_mu(r) = sum_p  coeff_p (x-X)^l (y-Y)^m (z-Z)^n exp(-ga |r-R|^2),

where coeff already contains the contraction coefficient and all normalisation factors of the electronic structure package the basis came from. Density matrices handed to the kernels must be expressed in the functions chi_mu, whatever their normalisation.

pyxscat.aligned¶

Scattering from molecules with a fixed orientation (aligned or oriented molecules, single detector images), at arbitrary scattering vectors q = (qx, qy, qz) in the molecular frame.

Because exp(i q.(r1 - r2)) = exp(i q.r1) exp(-i q.r2), every quantity follows from the Fourier transforms of basis-function products:

F_mu,nu(q) = int chi_mu(r) chi_nu(r) exp(i q.r) dr,

computed analytically (pair_transform) and contracted with the density matrices:

form factor     F(q)   = sum D_mu,nu F_mu,nu(q)                  elastic = |F(q)|^2
two-electron    I2(q)  = sum Gamma_{mu nu, la si} F_mu,nu F_la,si^*
total           I(q)   = N + I2(q)

The cost is one pass over primitive pairs plus dense linear algebra; there is no Bessel expansion, so close pair centres need no special treatment.

Detector geometry: ewald_q gives the scattering vectors for given scattering and azimuthal angles, and rotate_q turns laboratory-frame vectors into the molecular frame for a given molecular orientation.

pyxscat.grids¶

Scattering-vector grids for fixed-orientation and partially aligned calculations.

The polar detector grid is the one of qgrid3d.f90 (qcoord3) of the earlier code, kept point for point so that results line up with existing files:

  • n_rings rings of equally spaced transverse momentum q_perp = i * qxymax / n_rings, i = 1 .. n_rings, with qxymax = k0 sin(theta_max) (theta_max = largest scattering angle);

  • n_azimuth equally spaced azimuths per ring, phi_j = 2 pi j / n_azimuth, measured from the y axis: q_x = q_perp sin(phi), q_y = q_perp cos(phi);

  • q_z = k0 [1 - sqrt(1 - q_perp^2 / k0^2)] on the Ewald sphere (beam along z);

  • the point q = 0 first, then ring by ring, azimuth fastest: npts = n_rings * n_azimuth + 1 (in qcoord3’s arguments: n1 = n_rings, n2 = n_azimuth + 1).

Its sign convention is q = k_in - k_out (q_z >= 0); THISTLE’s square detector uses k_out - k_in. Scattering intensities are the same at q and -q (I(-q) = I(q) for real wavefunctions), and the P2 weight of an aligned ensemble is even, so the two conventions give identical patterns.

All q in bohr^-1 (atomic units).

pyxscat.electron¶

Electron scattering (ultrafast electron diffraction, gas electron diffraction).

In the first Born approximation a fast electron is scattered by the whole charge distribution of the molecule. With Z(q) = sum_A Z_A exp(i q.R_A) the nuclear and F(q) the electronic form factor, the differential cross section is:

dsigma/dOmega = (4 / a0^2 q^4) [ <|Z(q) - F(q)|^2> + S_inel(q) ]
              = (4 / a0^2 q^4) [ I_nn - 2 I_ne + I_ee ],

where I_ee is the total X-ray scattering (N + two-electron term) and:

I_nn(q) = sum_AB Z_A Z_B j0(q R_AB)                               nucleus-nucleus
I_ne(q) = sum_A Z_A int rho(r) j0(q |r - R_A|) dr                 electron-nucleus
elastic   I_nn - 2 I_ne + <|F|^2>
inelastic I_ee - <|F|^2>  (the same as for X-rays: the nuclei do not scatter inelastically)

The functions here return the bracket […] (“scattering in electron units”, the quantity of the earlier code, 0 at q = 0 for a neutral molecule); cross_section() multiplies by 4/q^4. Relativistic beam electrons: multiply by gamma^2 = (1 + E/mc^2)^2.

The electron-nucleus integrals are analytic (McMurchie-Davidson with the auxiliary functions (1/h d/dh)^n j0(qh)), in Fortran. l=2 gives the second Legendre moment about z, for aligned molecules: I(q, theta_q) = I0 + 5 <P2> P2(cos theta_q) I2, as for X-rays.

q in bohr^-1, coordinates in bohr.

pyxscat.iam¶

Independent atom model (IAM), for X-rays and electrons: the reference against which molecular (bonding, correlation) effects are measured.

The molecule is modelled as free, spherically averaged atoms at the nuclear positions:

elastic     I_el(q)  = sum_AB f_A(q) f_B(q) j0(q R_AB)                 (rotational average)
                     = |sum_A f_A(q) exp(i q.R_A)|^2                   (fixed orientation)
total       I_tot(q) = sum_A S_A(q) + sum_{A != B} f_A f_B j0(q R_AB)   (or cos(q.R_AB))
inelastic   I_tot - I_el = sum_A [S_A(q) - f_A(q)^2]

f_A is the form factor of the spherically averaged atomic density and S_A = N_A + I2_A the total (elastic + inelastic) scattering of the free atom. Two sources:

  • source="ab initio" (default): free-atom calculations (basis is a PySCF basis name, or a dict element -> basis name) with PySCF at a chosen level (UHF by default, or CCSD for closed-shell atoms) and basis, through the same kernels as the molecule. Elastic and total. With the molecule’s own method and basis, molecule minus IAM isolates the effect of bonding.

  • source="tables": Waasmaier-Kirfel form factors (through the xraydb package). Elastic only; the tables give no incoherent scattering.

q is in inverse bohr throughout.

pyxscat.ci¶

Reduced density matrices from configuration-interaction vectors.

Pure numpy and independent of any electronic structure package. A CI wavefunction is given as Slater determinants over norb active orbitals, each as a pair of occupation bit strings (bit k set = orbital k occupied), with the determinant defined as:

|alpha string, beta string> = a+_{a1 alpha} a+_{a2 alpha} ... a+_{b1 beta} a+_{b2 beta} ... |0>

(all alpha creation operators first, each set in ascending orbital order; PySCF’s convention).

Spin-summed densities follow PySCF: dm1[p, q] = <E_pq>, dm2[p, q, r, s] = <p+ r+ s q> (chemists’ notation, trace N(N-1)), with E_pq = sum_sigma a+_{p sigma} a_{q sigma}.

pyxscat.ci.ci_matrix(norb, nalpha, nbeta, dets, coeffs)[source]¶

Arrange determinant coefficients in the alpha x beta string matrix (full active space).

pyxscat.ci.make_rdm12(norb, nalpha, nbeta, dets, coeffs, normalize=True, method='auto')[source]¶

Spin-summed 1- and 2-particle density matrices of a determinant expansion.

dets is a sequence of (alpha_bits, beta_bits) integers, coeffs the CI coefficients. Returns dm1 (norb, norb) and dm2 (norb, norb, norb, norb).

Three exact builders (they agree to 1e-15):

"dense"    dm2[p,q,r,s] = <E_qp Psi|E_rs Psi> - delta_qr dm1[p,s] with the intermediates
               x_rs = E_rs|Psi> over the complete active space (alpha x beta strings, in
               blocks); cost ~ norb^4 N_FCI in BLAS.  Best for CASSCF/CASCI vectors.
"bitwise"  the determinant-pair algorithm of the earlier code (Fortran, OpenMP): pairs
               screened with popcnt(ieor), Slater-Condon rules for those differing by at
               most two spin-orbitals; cost ~ N_det^2 (a few instructions per pair),
               independent of N_FCI.  Best for truncated or selected expansions (MCCI, a
               print threshold, RAS) in large orbital spaces.  Any number of orbitals
               (the only builder beyond 64: multiword bit strings).
"sparse"   the x_rs intermediates only on the determinants reached from the given ones
               (sparse product, SciPy); cost ~ N_det norb N_el.  Linear in N_det: for
               very long expansions on few cores.
"auto"     the cheapest by an estimate of the three costs.
pyxscat.ci.make_rdm12_external(norb, nint, dets, coeffs, normalize=True, nthreads=None, max_external=2)[source]¶

RDMs of an expansion in which at most max_external (2) electrons occupy the “external” orbitals nint, ..., norb - 1 (uncontracted MRCI, CISD-like expansions): the pair loop is restricted to determinant pairs that can couple, and the block of the 2RDM with four external indices is formed by matrix products instead.

Determinants are grouped by their external label (the set of external spin-orbitals). Two determinants couple only if the labels differ by at most four spin-orbitals; with exactly four the internal parts are identical and only the external-external-external- external block of dm2 receives a contribution,

Gamma[c,a,d,b] = sum_K sum_{sigma tau} T_K^{sigma tau}[c,d] T_K^{sigma tau}[a,b],

where T_K^{sigma tau}[a,b] is the (signed) coefficient of the determinant with internal part K and externals (a sigma, b tau). So the kernel runs over pairs whose labels differ by at most three spin-orbitals (orders of magnitude fewer than all pairs) and skips that block, which is then added from the products. Exact; any number of orbitals.

pyxscat.ci.factor_active(dm2_act, tol=1e-12)[source]¶

Active 2RDM as a sum of products, Gamma[p,q,r,s] = sum_k w_k V_k[p,q] V_k[r,s].

Gamma is averaged over its 8 index permutations (the scattering only sees that part) and diagonalised as a symmetric (pq) x (rs) matrix; eigenvalues below tol x the largest are dropped. At most n_act (n_act + 1) / 2 terms. Returns (w, V) with V of shape (K, na, na).

pyxscat.ci.embed_core(dm1_act, dm2_act, ncore, nmo=None, factored=False, tol=1e-12)[source]¶

Add ncore doubly occupied orbitals in front of an active space; optionally pad with empty orbitals up to nmo.

factored=True returns the 2RDM as a pyxscat.core.FactoredDM2 instead of the n^4 array: core-core and core-active blocks as products of the core and active 1RDMs (3 Coulomb + 3 exchange terms) and the active block from factor_active(). pyxscat.ccsd¶

The CCSD two-particle density as a sum of products, without the four-index array.

PySCF’s make_rdm2 builds the full nmo^4 (then nao^4) tensor, several copies of it: a molecule of 114 basis functions already needs 6 GB. The density has structure, though: every block of the cumulant carries occupied indices except the virtual-virtual-virtual-virtual one, and that one is exactly a sum over occupied pairs of products,

Gamma_abcd (vvvv) = sum_ij lambda2_ij,ac tau_ij,bd (+ permutations), tau = t2 + t1 t1,

so the whole density can be written as pyxscat.core.FactoredDM2,

Gamma[mu,nu,la,si] = sum_k Ac_k[mu,nu] Bc_k[la,si] + sum_k Ax_k[mu,la] Bx_k[nu,si],

with K = 7 nocc^2 + 4 terms: memory 2 K nao^2 instead of nao^4 (benzene/cc-pVDZ: 0.6 GB against 1.35 GB for the array alone; 500 functions, 40 occupied: 45 GB against 360 GB). The scattering kernels contract such a density directly (kernel mode 3).

The terms reproduce PySCF’s ccsd_rdm._make_rdm2 (with the one-particle terms, in chemists’ notation, trace N(N-1)) exactly when contracted with any kernel that is symmetric under the 8 index permutations, which all scattering kernels are; the intermediates of passes 1 and 2 of PySCF’s _gamma2_outcore (BSD licence, Q. Sun et al.) are built here in memory (they are at most nocc^2 nvir^2), its pass 3 (the nvir^4 and nocc nvir^3 blocks) is replaced by the factorisation. Checked against make_rdm2 to 1e-13 (tests/test_ccsd_factored.py).
