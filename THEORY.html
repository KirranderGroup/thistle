THISTLE: what is computed, and how¶

Authors: Andrés Moreno Carrascosa, Mats Simmermacher, Patrick Yuheng Wang, Eirik Moen Liane, Joseph Cooper, Nikola Zotev, Thomas Northey and Adam Kirrander.

THISTLE (with its kernel library pyxscat) computes the scattering of X-rays and fast electrons by molecules directly from their electronic wavefunction, given as a Gaussian basis set and one- and two-particle density matrices (1RDM, 2RDM). It does not depend on any electronic structure program: PySCF objects, Molden files, OpenMolcas RASSCF and Molpro MULTI outputs are read by thin adapters, and any other source only needs to provide a basis and density matrices.

Atomic units throughout unless stated (q in bohr⁻¹, lengths in bohr). The user interface converts to Å⁻¹ and Å² by default.

1. X-ray scattering¶

1.1 Total, elastic and inelastic scattering¶

For a molecule in electronic state Ψ, the X-ray scattering in electron units (Thomson cross section = 1), summed over all final electronic states (the closure or Waller–Hartree approximation, valid when the photon energy greatly exceeds the electronic excitation energies), is

I_tot(q) = ⟨Ψ| Σ_ij exp(iq·(r_i − r_j)) |Ψ⟩ = N + ∫∫ Γ(r₁, r₂) exp(iq·(r₁ − r₂)) dr₁ dr₂,

with N the number of electrons and Γ the spin-summed pair density (normalised to N(N−1)). The elastic part is the square of the form factor F(q) = ∫ ρ(r) exp(iq·r) dr,

I_el(q) = |F(q)|²,         I_inel(q) = I_tot(q) − I_el(q).

In the Gaussian basis {χ_μ}, with ρ = Σ D_μν χ_μ χ_ν and Γ = Σ Γ_μνλσ χ_μ χ_ν (1) χ_λ χ_σ (2) (chemists’ order, PySCF’s make_rdm2 convention),

F(q)  = Σ_μν D_μν F_μν(q),       F_μν(q) = ∫ χ_μ χ_ν exp(iq·r) dr,
I₂(q) = Σ_μνλσ Γ_μνλσ ⟨μν|exp(iq·r₁₂)|λσ⟩.

1.2 Decomposition¶

Writing Γ = D⊗D − ½ D⊗D (exchange) + Λ, with Λ the cumulant (the genuinely correlated part),

I_tot = N + I_Hartree + I_exchange + I_cumulant,     I_el = I_Hartree,

where the first three terms are built from the 1RDM alone. For a closed-shell Hartree–Fock determinant Λ = 0, so total scattering follows from the orbitals (hf_total, --hf). For natural orbitals of correlated wavefunctions it does not, which is why a Molden file alone only gives elastic scattering unless the user declares it Hartree–Fock.

Coupled cluster without the four-index density. The CCSD 2RDM of PySCF is an n_MO⁴ then n_AO⁴ array, several copies of it: benzene/cc-pVDZ (120 Cartesian functions) needs more than 6 GB, 500 functions would need 360 GB for the array alone. Its cumulant has structure: every block carries occupied indices except the all-virtual one, and that block is exactly a sum over occupied pairs of products,

Λ_abcd (vvvv) = Σ_ij λ₂^{ij}_{ac} (τ^{ij} − ½ τ^{ij,T})_{bd} (+ images),    τ = t₂ + t₁t₁,

and the blocks with one occupied index (ovvv) are sums of products of the λ- and t-amplitudes with one summed occupied index. The whole density is therefore written, exactly, as

Γ_μνλσ = Σ_k A^C_k(μν) B^C_k(λσ)  +  Σ_k A^X_k(μλ) B^X_k(νσ),

with K_C = 2 n_occ² + 2 Coulomb-paired and K_X = 5 n_occ² + 2 exchange-paired terms (the one-particle part is 4 P⊗(D − P) − 2 P⊗(D − P)_exchange with P the occupied projector), each an n_AO × n_AO matrix: memory 2(K_C + K_X) n_AO² instead of n_AO⁴ (benzene 0.7 GB; 500 functions and 40 occupied orbitals 45 GB). The intermediates are those of PySCF’s ccsd_rdm (passes 1 and 2, at most n_occ² n_vir²); pass 3, which builds the n_vir⁴ and n_occ n_vir³ blocks, is replaced by the factorisation. The kernels contract this form directly (mode 3): the Coulomb-paired terms in the canonical shell-quartet loop (their block is 8-fold symmetric by itself), the exchange-paired ones in a pass ordered by the (μ,λ) shell pair, where the block of a batch of shell quartets is built from eight matrix products over k (BLAS dgemm, taken from SciPy at run time) of factor blocks shared across the batch. It reproduces the dense route to 10⁻¹³ (every block of the MO density, and total, j₂, fixed-orientation and electron scattering; frozen core included) and is the default beyond 60 basis functions. Timings on 2 cores: water/cc-pVTZ 4.3 s against 2.2 s dense; benzene/cc-pVDZ 120 s in 2.4 GB, where the dense route does not fit in 6 GB. The same representation serves any method whose 2RDM is given as products of one-particle matrices.

1.3 Rotational average (gas phase)¶

For randomly oriented molecules, exp(iq·r) averages to j₀(qr) = sin(qr)/(qr):

⟨I(q)⟩ = N + Σ Γ_μνλσ ⟨μν|j₀(q r₁₂)|λσ⟩.

Evaluation. Each primitive product χ_a χ_b is expanded in Hermite Gaussians at its centre P with exponent p (McMurchie–Davidson; p’ and Q for the second pair). The Hermite coefficients come from the usual recurrence, valid for any angular momentum. A primitive quartet then reduces to derivatives of j₀(q|P − Q|) with respect to the vector between the two pair centres:

⟨ab|j₀(q r₁₂)|cd⟩ = (π²/pp')^{3/2} exp(−q²/4p − q²/4p') Σ E^{ab}_{tuv} E^{cd}_{τνφ} (−1)^{τ+ν+φ}
                    ∂^{t+τ}_x ∂^{u+ν}_y ∂^{v+φ}_z j₀(q|h|) at h = P − Q.

The Cartesian derivatives of j₀ are combinations Σ_b c_b j_b(qh)(q/h)^b = Σ_b c_b q^{2b} g_b(qh) with g_b(x) = j_b(x)/x^b (b up to 4l_max + 2). Each order must be accurate for every x, and no single recurrence direction achieves that: the upward recurrence is stable only for orders b < x, the downward one for b > x. The radial functions are therefore evaluated as follows:

Coincident centres need no special treatment; the closed form

∂^{L}_x ∂^{M}_y ∂^{N}_z j₀(q|h|) at h = 0 = (−1)^{S/2} q^S (L−1)!!(M−1)!!(N−1)!!/(S+1)!!,  S = L+M+N even,

is still used for h < cutoffcentre (10⁻¹⁰ bohr), where it is exact to machine precision.

Density providers. The quartet loop is the same for every quantity; only the weights change: an explicit 2RDM, or products built on the fly from one-particle matrices (Z = w_c/2 (A⊗B + B⊗A) + w_x A⊗A exchange). This gives elastic, transition and coherent cross terms (w_c = 1, w_x = 0), exchange (w_c = 0, w_x = −1/4) and the Hartree–Fock mean field (w_c = 1, w_x = −1/4) without storing any four-index array.

Efficiency. This is the “one-loop” algorithm of the earlier code (the latest version, Total_scattering_one_loop_opt.f90): a canonical loop over contracted-shell quartets with the primitive quartets inside, so each block of the pair density is gathered once per shell quartet; quartets are screened by bounds on Gaussian prefactors and density weights (screen_tol, 10⁻¹² by default); the outer loop is parallelised with OpenMP. The cost scales as the number of significant primitive quartets × the number of q points.

Elastic scattering by density-weighted pairs. For the elastic term (and coherent/transition terms) the pair density is a product, D⊗D, and a much cheaper route exists. The density is folded into every primitive-shell pair first, turning it into one Hermite charge distribution at its centre P,

W_P(tuv) = Σ_{a∈P, b∈P} c_a c_b D_ab E^{ab}_{tuv},

and the elastic scattering becomes a generalised Debye sum over pairs of such distributions,

⟨|F(q)|²⟩ = Σ_{P,Q} pref_P pref_Q e^{−q²/4p − q²/4p'} Σ_m S_m(P − Q) (−q²)^m j_m(qh)/(qh)^m,

S_m = Σ_{TUV} U_{TUV} Σ_{ijk: T+U+V−i−j−k=m} a_{Ti} a_{Uj} a_{Vk} h_x^{T−2i} h_y^{U−2j} h_z^{V−2k},
U_{TUV} = Σ W_P(tuv) W_Q(t'u'v') (−1)^{t'+u'+v'}  (t+t' = T, …),   a_{Ti} = T!/(i!(T−2i)! 2^i),

where the second line is the closed form of the Cartesian derivatives of a radial function (f_n = (1/h d/dh)^n f). Per pair of pairs the work is one convolution of two Hermite tables, a q-independent radial sum and, for each q, one set of j_m(x)/x^m (evaluated as above). Because |∂^{tuv} j₀(q|h|)| ≤ q^{t+u+v}, the contribution at each q is rigorously bounded by pref_P pref_Q e^{−q²/4p−q²/4p’} Σ_m |U_m| q^m, which drops the q tail of diffuse pairs. Nothing is special at coincident or nearly coincident pair centres, so there is no cutoffcentre approximation. This route is the default for elastic scattering:

1.4 Partially aligned molecules: the j₂ moment¶

For molecules aligned along a laboratory axis (laser alignment, with axial symmetry), the signal at a scattering vector making the angle θ_q with that axis is

I(q, θ_q) = I₀(q) + 5 ⟨P₂(cos β)⟩ P₂(cos θ_q) S₂(q) + (l = 4, …),

with ⟨P₂⟩ the degree of alignment and

S₂(q) = i² Σ Γ ⟨μν| j₂(q r₁₂) P₂(cos θ₁₂) |λσ⟩ = −Σ Γ ⟨μν| j₂(q r₁₂) P₂(cos θ₁₂) |λσ⟩,

θ₁₂ being the angle of r₁₂ with the molecular z axis. The factor i² = −1 is the phase of the l = 2 term of the plane-wave expansion, e^{iq·r} = Σ_l (2l+1) iˡ j_l(qr) P_l(q̂·r̂); with it included every moment enters with a plus sign, I = Σ_l (2l+1) ⟨P_l⟩ P_l(cos θ_q) S_l, and S₂ is directly the projection S₂ = ⟨I(q) P₂(cos θ_q)⟩ over directions of q for perfect alignment (= a₂/5, a₂ the l = 2 Legendre coefficient). (Before 8 October 2026 pyxscat returned −S₂, with I = I₀ − 5⟨P₂⟩P₂S₂; the numbers are the same up to that sign.) The j₂P₂ kernel is reduced to j₀ by Hobson’s theorem,

j₂(qh) P₂(cos θ_h) = [3 ∂²/∂h_z² + q²] j₀(qh) / (2q²),

so the same derivative machinery serves (two more orders), with closed-form coincident limits. The isotropic self term N does not contribute to S₂. The convention of the earlier code (with Y₂₀ and without i²) differs by a factor −√(5/4π).

1.5 Fixed orientation (oriented molecules, single-shot detector images)¶

Since exp(iq·(r₁ − r₂)) = exp(iq·r₁) exp(−iq·r₂), all fixed-orientation quantities follow from the analytic pair transforms

F_ab(q) = (π/p)^{3/2} exp(−q²/4p) exp(iq·P) Σ E^{ab}_{tuv} (iq_x)^t (iq_y)^u (iq_z)^v,

contracted with density matrices: elastic |Σ D F|², two-electron Σ Γ F F* (one matrix product per q), exchange and mean field from the 1RDM at O(n³) per q. There is no Bessel expansion and no coincident-centre special case. Detector geometry: q vectors on the Ewald sphere for a given wavelength (beam along z), and molecular orientations as z-y-z Euler angles.

1.6 Detector grids and aligned ensembles in the laboratory¶

The beam runs along z with wavenumber k₀ (k₀ = E·0.26817 bohr⁻¹/keV for photons; relativistic 2π/λ for electrons), and the scattered wave vectors lie on the Ewald sphere. Besides a square (q_x, q_y) image, results can be computed on the polar grid of the earlier grid.f90 (qcoord3), reproduced point for point: n₁ rings of transverse momentum q⊥,i = i·k₀ sin θ_max / n₁, n_φ equally spaced azimuths per ring (φ from the y axis: q_x = q⊥ sin φ, q_y = q⊥ cos φ), q_z = k₀[1 − (1 − q⊥²/k₀²)^{1/2}], and q = 0 first (N = n₁ n_φ + 1 points; grid.f90’s n₂ is n_φ + 1). Its sign is q = k_in − k_out; since I(−q) = I(q) for real wavefunctions and the alignment weight below is even, the patterns are the same in either convention. Any other list of laboratory vectors can be read from a file (columns q_x q_y q_z in bohr⁻¹).

Fixed orientation. For Euler angles (α, β, γ), q_mol = Rᵀ q_lab and §1.5 applies point by point.

Partially aligned ensemble. For an alignment axis â (e.g. the laser polarisation) and a degree of alignment ⟨P₂(cos β)⟩ of the molecular z axis, §1.4 gives at every grid point

I(q) = I₀(|q|) + 5 ⟨P₂⟩ P₂(q̂·â) S₂(|q|),

with I₀ and S₂ computed once per distinct |q| (once per ring on the polar grid). Terms l ≥ 4 (⟨P₄⟩ S₄, …) are neglected; the relation is exact for any distribution with no components beyond l = 2, e.g. f(β) ∝ 1 + 5⟨P₂⟩P₂(cos β). The same holds for electron cross sections.

IAM on the same grid. At fixed orientation the IAM is |Σ_A f_A(q) e^{iq·R_A}|² plus the atomic inelastic terms (electrons: amplitudes Z_A − f_A); for the aligned ensemble its I₀ and S₂ are the Debye forms of §3 at l = 0 and 2. Molecule and IAM are thus compared point by point, as 100 (I − I_IAM)/I_IAM.

2. Electron scattering¶

2.1 Cross sections¶

A fast electron is scattered by the whole charge distribution, nuclei and electrons. In the first Born approximation, with Z(q) = Σ_A Z_A exp(iq·R_A) the nuclear form factor,

dσ/dΩ = (4 / a₀² q⁴) [ |Z(q) − F(q)|² + I_inel(q) ],

where I_inel is the same inelastic term as for X-rays (nuclei do not scatter inelastically at these energies). Expanding the square,

dσ/dΩ = (4 / a₀² q⁴) [ I_nn − 2 I_ne + I_ee ],

I_nn = Σ_AB Z_A Z_B exp(iq·R_AB)                (nucleus–nucleus)
I_ne = Σ_A Z_A Re[exp(−iq·R_A) F(q)]             (electron–nucleus)
I_ee = I_tot (X-ray, total) or |F|² (elastic)    (electron–electron)

The code reports the bracket (as the earlier code did) at the kernel level and dσ/dΩ in the interface (Å²/sr for q in Å⁻¹; bohr²/sr for q in bohr⁻¹). For a neutral molecule the bracket vanishes at q = 0; for a polar molecule the elastic bracket starts as q² μ²/3 (μ the dipole moment), so dσ/dΩ diverges as 1/q² and the q = 0 point is undefined. For fast beams, the relativistic correction multiplies the cross section by γ² = (1 + E/mc²)²; the wavelength λ = h/p is relativistic as well (electron_wavelength).

2.2 Rotational average and the electron–nucleus integrals¶

On rotational averaging, I_nn = Σ Z_A Z_B j₀(qR_AB) and

I_ne(q) = Σ_A Z_A Σ_μν D_μν ∫ χ_μ χ_ν j₀(q|r − R_A|) dr.

This one-electron integral is evaluated analytically. With the Hermite expansion of χ_a χ_b at P,

∫ Λ_tuv(r − P) j₀(q|r − A|) dr = (π/p)^{3/2} exp(−q²/4p) ∂^t_x ∂^u_y ∂^v_z j₀(q|h|),  h = P − A,

and the derivatives of the radial function follow from the McMurchie–Davidson recursion

R^n_{000} = f_n(h),   R^n_{t+1,u,v} = t R^{n+1}_{t−1,u,v} + h_x R^{n+1}_{t,u,v}   (and y, z),

with the auxiliary functions

f_n(h) = (1/h d/dh)^n j₀(qh) = (−q²)^n j_n(qh)/(qh)^n.

The functions j_n(x)/x^n are evaluated by their power series for x < 1, by Miller’s backward recurrence for 1 ≤ x ≤ n_max + 1 and by upward recurrence beyond, so they are accurate for every x including x = 0. Nothing is singular when a nucleus sits on a pair centre. The j₂P₂ moment for aligned molecules uses Hobson’s theorem as in §1.4, and I_nn at l = 2 is −Σ Z_A Z_B j₂(qR_AB) P₂(cos θ_AB). The relation I(q, θ_q) = I₀ + 5⟨P₂⟩P₂(cos θ_q) S₂ holds term by term, so it holds for electrons as for X-rays.

The cost is one pass over primitive pairs × atoms × q points, negligible next to the two-electron terms (0.7 s for benzene/cc-pVDZ at 200 q on two cores, against 30 s for the X-ray elastic term).

2.3 Fixed orientation¶

At fixed orientation, Z(q) is a sum of plane waves and F(q) comes from the pair transforms of §1.5, so |Z − F|² and the inelastic term are exact for any list of q vectors (detector images for UED, given the beam energy).

2.4 Modified scattering intensity sM(s)¶

Electron diffraction experiments report the modified intensity

sM(s) = s (I − I_at) / I_at,      s = q,

where I_at is the incoherent sum of atomic scattering. THISTLE gives it for the ab initio molecule (with I_at from the independent atom model below) and for the IAM itself (s I_mol/I_at, the usual simulation). The difference is the effect of chemical bonding and electron correlation on the diffraction pattern.

3. Independent atom model (IAM)¶

The reference against which molecular effects are measured: free, spherically averaged atoms at the nuclear positions.

X-rays     I_el = Σ_AB f_A f_B j₀(qR_AB),     I_tot = Σ_A S_A + Σ_{A≠B} f_A f_B j₀(qR_AB)
electrons  I_el = Σ_AB (Z_A − f_A)(Z_B − f_B) j₀(qR_AB),   + Σ_A (S_A − f_A²) inelastic

(the electron amplitude Z_A − f_A is the Mott–Bethe formula). At fixed orientation j₀(qR) becomes exp(iq·R_AB); for l = 2, j₂(qR_AB) P₂(cos θ_AB).

The atomic form factors f_A and total scattering S_A = N_A + I₂,A come either from ab initio free-atom calculations through the same kernels (UHF or closed-shell CCSD, in a chosen basis per element; the spherical average is exact on a Lebedev grid of degree 17 because the angular structure of an atomic density is a polynomial of low degree), or from the Waasmaier–Kirfel tables (elastic only). Using the molecule’s own method and basis isolates the effect of bonding.

Exact limits are tested throughout: I_tot(0) = I_el(0) = N², I_ne(0) = Σ Z_A · N, the decompositions sum to the totals, and electron brackets vanish at q = 0 for neutral molecules. The suite (python -m pytest, about 5 minutes) runs 217 tests; PYXSCAT_SLOW=1 adds 5 on large basis sets.

6. Limits and plans¶

  • Angular momentum: tested up to h functions (l = 5) in every kernel. The quartet kernel’s derivative tables are dimensioned for 4 l_max + 3 ≤ 32 (l ≤ 7); the pair, electron–nucleus and fixed-orientation kernels have no fixed limit. The Molden format defines shells only up to g, so h functions come in through PySCF (or any adapter that supplies the basis directly).

  • Closure (Waller–Hartree) and first Born approximations; no Compton energy resolution, no multiple scattering.

  • The j₂ moment is computed through Hobson’s theorem, [3∂²_z + q²] j₀/(2q²), which cancels as q → 0: with the stable radial functions the relative error is 10⁻¹¹ at q = 0.01 bohr⁻¹ and 4·10⁻⁸ at 10⁻³ (the elastic j₂ through the pair route: 10⁻¹³).

  • j₂ / aligned ensembles: S₂ is the moment about the molecular z axis of the input frame (the laboratory alignment axis is free). A molecule whose aligned axis is not z must be reoriented before the quantum-chemistry run; nothing warns about it. To do: a molecular_axis option (rotate basis and densities, exact for Cartesian Gaussians) or the full l = 2 tensor T (S₂(n) = n·T·n, five components, any axis afterwards). Only one-dimensional alignment (⟨P₂⟩ of one axis); 3D alignment of asymmetric tops would need higher terms.

  • Planned: j₁ terms; BAGEL and NEXMD adapters. (Factored CCSD and CAS/CI densities: done.)
